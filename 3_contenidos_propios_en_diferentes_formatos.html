Reading comprehension exercise

What is a digital detox? And do you really need one?


Have you ever looked at your phone the moment you wake up? Have you scrolled through social media without really knowing why or felt anxious when your battery died? If any of this sounds familiar, you are not alone. Teenagers today spend an average of six to seven hours per day in front of screens. That is more time than they spend sleeping.

A digital detox is a period of time when you deliberately reduce or stop using digital devices (smartphones, tablets, computers) and social media. It does not mean throwing your phone in the sea. It means making a conscious choice to disconnect, even for a short time, so that you can reconnect with yourself and the people around you. Why does it matter? Research from the National Health Service (NHS, UK) shows that excessive screen time is linked to poor sleep quality, difficulties concentrating, and higher levels of anxiety among young people. Every time you get a notification, your brain releases a small amount of dopamine, the same chemical linked to pleasure and reward. This is why checking your phone can feel so satisfying, and why it is so hard to stop.

However, a digital detox does not have to be dramatic. Small changes can make a real difference. Experts suggest starting with a "no-screens hour" before bed, turning off notifications during meals, or spending at least thirty minutes outdoors without your phone each day. These habits are sometimes called micro-detoxes, small breaks that add up over time.

The goal is not to demonise technology. Smartphones are useful, creative and social tools. The goal is to develop a healthy relationship with technology, one where you choose when to use it, rather than feeling you have no choice at all. So, the next time you reach for your phone out of habit, pause for a second. Ask yourself: Am I using this, or is it using me?
